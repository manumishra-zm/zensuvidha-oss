One call, end to end

The wide path is the common one. Everything indented is an exception that only fires when its condition is met.

MicrophonegetUserMedia

Echo cancellation and noise suppression on; automatic gain off — AGC rescales the very signal the VAD's noise floor adapts to.

AudioWorkletvad-worklet.js off the main thread

Splits into two paths: raw PCM goes to the server untouched, while a 2-pole biquad at 300–3400 Hz feeds only the level gate — 2.79× noise rejection. Whisper never sees band-limited audio.

Silero VAD v5ONNX-WASM · 2.3 MB

Answers "is this speech?" — never "whose?". Needs the previous frame's last 64 samples prepended, or it scores 0.11 on clear speech instead of 0.99, and looks fine doing it.

The speaker gate, and why it is so cautious

It answers "is this the person whose call this is?". It has caused more user-visible failures than anything else here, and every fix has moved it toward refusing less.

The measurement that explains all of them. 
macOS say voices   same speaker 0.867   closest impostor 0.429   → 0.55 works
a real microphone    same speaker 0.27–0.41                        → 0.55 refuses them
The threshold was calibrated on synthetic audio. On a real mic, in a room measured clean, a caller's own consecutive turns scored 0.37 / 0.27 / 0.29 / 0.41 — never once above it.

So two independent conditions must hold before anyone is refused:

  • proven — it has matched this caller at least once on this call. A gate that has never recognised them has no evidence it can, and refusing on that is a coin toss that silences whoever is actually on the phone.
  • corroborated — the print is more than one un-corroborated sample.

While either is missing, a mismatch is answered and the voice remembered as a rival. A rival that returns twice becomes the caller — the voice having the conversation is the caller, and a song does not follow up on its own question.

Our own trimming was refusing the caller. _voiced_only used to splice out the gaps between words, and every join is an artificial transient the encoder hears as part of the voice. It scored the caller 0.450 against their own sentence — below threshold. Keeping the voiced span gives 0.843. The tell was a metric that got worse with more audio: 3.0 s → 0.507 against 2.0 s → 0.818.

Noise — rejection-first, not reduction-first

Of the layers that touch noise, only two modify the signal. The rest refuse to act.

Auto-denoise is OFF, and this is measured. DeepFilterNet across six interference types × five SNRs — 30 conditions: 
DeepFilter WON 1 cell, LOST 8, tied 21      net −400% word recall
white hiss −50%   ·   fan/AC −67%   ·   music+vocals −100%
It is also the most expensive stage at 226–475 ms. On the recognition path it costs time and accuracy — the same verdict noisereduce got. Whisper trained on 680k hours of messy audio.

What it survives

Caller-relative SNR: +12 = you are 12 dB louder than the noise, 0 = equal, −6 = the noise is twice as loud as you. "Works" means the words survived and the caller was still recognised.

Two patterns. Low-frequency and tonal interference is nearly free; broadband is expensive. And anything containing a human voice takes out the speaker gate before it takes out the transcript — the words usually survive, it is recognising them as yours that fails.

The audio path is STT-bound. Isolation is ~6 % of it. Four attempts to speed it further were measured and rejected: ECAPA on MPS fails outright (SpeechBrain keeps CPU tensors); batching the thirds gate is a real 1.5× on a 60 ms stage; cpu_threads is worse with more threads on Apple silicon (auto 1691 · 8 threads 2212 · 10 threads 3798 ms); and STT fast mode is not reliably faster.

Indic latency is tokenizer inflation, not runtime. At 38.7 tok/s a 40-token reply is 1.03 s in English, 3.62 s in Hindi, 6.41 s in Telugu. No inference library fixes that — which is why MLX was benchmarked and rejected. The GPU preset is the answer, and it also fixes the five mute languages.

Three things that look wasteful and are not

The 6,089-token system prompt. It is load-bearing twice: the whole knowledge base sits in a byte-identical prefix so Ollama reuses its KV cache (640 ms steady-state turns), and the full English KB is what lets Hindi and Telugu callers get answers at all — English tags cannot retrieve against a native-script query. Trimming it breaks both.
Silero running twice. v5 in the browser answers "should I open a turn?"; v6 on the server answers "what should Whisper see?". Different questions — and the second copy ships free inside faster-whisper.
Two protocol messages that do nothing on a good call. commit_miss and turn_dropped exist so the call can never wedge: the client enters "thinking" the moment it ships audio and only leaves on a reply, so every path that declines to answer must say so. Silence is often the right reply; it is never the right protocol.

What is not solved

  • Fan/AC needs +12 dB. You must be four times louder than a ceiling fan — the commonest Indian-SMB condition — and denoising cannot rescue it (measured −67 %).
  • Five languages have no voice on macOS: Malayalam, Gujarati, Punjabi, Odia, Urdu. The caller sees the reply and hears nothing. Warned at boot; fixed only by the GPU preset with Indic-Parler.
  • Loud audio at the mic destroys the voiceprint (0.07 on the caller's own voice). Mitigated — the latch guard cuts it and asks for a repeat — not solved.
  • No echo cancellation without a browser. All AEC comes from getUserMedia. A telephony transport has none, so the agent would hear itself and interrupt itself on a loop. A level-based guard is in place; real server-side AEC is needed before the phone line.
  • Every remaining threshold is calibrated on synthetic speech. A real microphone has already proved one wrong by more than 2×. Real recordings are the single highest-value missing input.
  • No LICENSE file, and XTTS — a selectable provider — is non-commercial and undocumented as such.
